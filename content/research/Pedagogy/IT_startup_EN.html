Information Technology in the Development of the University
Products: specialists, science and technology

Products: specialists, science and technology

As it develops, humanity has to deal with more complex and global problems (tasks) such as inefficient use of natural resources, demographic, climatic and environmental problems, etc. [13], the solution of which requires more sophisticated technical means and technologies. In the 21st century, very sophisticated technologies, most often including electronics and robotics, used in manufacturing and other industries — the so-called innovative high technologies — have gained the most development [7]. The basis of such technologies lies in science-intensive, resource-saving and information technology (IT). Examples are microelectronics, software, communication, robotics, nanotechnology, biotechnology, security systems, wireless technologies, environmentally friendly technologies, energy saving, alternative energy, etc.

As we can see, IT is directly or indirectly connected with all high technologies for the production of goods with high added value. Computerization permeates all spheres of life of society: not only the production of goods and services, but also the domestic economy (household), as well as culture and art [14, 15].

New technologies are increasingly being introduced into human life processes and technological business processes, the share of automated links is increasing, many technological processes are fully automated — they are performed without human participation, mechanical parts are replaced by microelectronics, robots and computerized complexes are widely used, and new high-tech industries are emerging.

A high-tech company, enterprise, organization, corporation, etc. (hereinafter referred to as the company), operating in a competitive market, produces high-tech products or performs high-tech services, attracting highly qualified specialists. The high-tech process is effective, including in terms of such a resource as the number of specialists. That is, the higher the level of technology, the lower the need for people involved in this technology. But, nevertheless, it will not do without a specialist. A high-tech process, like any system, has a human interface with corresponding responsibilities [11, 12], and a person as a specialist must fulfill these responsibilities (interface). A specialist with such responsibilities is an integral part of a high-tech process. The ability to perform these duties qualitatively and efficiently is the competence of a specialist; in fact, a person with such competencies is considered a specialist. A person usually gets the necessary qualifications at an institution of higher education (hereinafter referred to as university).

From today's requirements for the development of society, it follows that today the university is obliged to form and permanently enrich a high level of competence of the elite layer of society, a high culture of thinking and high-tech activity, create and disseminate universal human social and cultural values, and develop societies [3].

Due to the widespread coverage of human activities with high-tech automated processes, the demand for physical work decreases and, conversely, for highly skilled mental work — competent specialists in high-tech automated processes — increases. Knowledge becomes the main public resource [16] and an influential factor in the fourth industrial revolution [17]. Personnel training: training and education, advanced training, retraining and requalification are becoming increasingly priority. The university is becoming the main driving force of society.

Bearing in mind the aforementioned state of the development of society and the situation existing in our Republic, what kind of analysis can be done, what primary tasks should the university solve, and where should we start? At first glance, we see such a paradoxical situation: in order to ensure the employment of highly qualified personnel, the existence of high-tech industries is necessary, but for the normal functioning of high-tech industries, appropriate personnel are required. It is clear that personnel and company are closely interconnected and develop each other in a spiral and mutually, but where to start, which is primary: production or personnel?

Today, in our country, the investment climate is very good and attractive for internal and external investors [18]. The creation of new high-tech manufacturing enterprises and the transformation of existing enterprises into high-tech ones, and the implementation of innovations in life are the priority tasks of our State [19]. The investment climate, investment and production built on their basis will not bring the desired benefits without highly qualified specialists and competent personnel. Therefore, training and education are primary tasks. At the same time, higher education should not only integrate closely with theoretical and applied science, but also closely cooperate with production. Not only collaborate, but also create new high-tech companies based on university resources.

Our government pays special attention to the integration of education and production. The government should continue to provide financial support in the direction of creating such production technologies, where the university plays the role of an innovation center. This is a very important factor in the initial phase of the transformation of the university. Moreover, the need to weaken the strong dependence of the university on the center and on the excessive management of the Ministry of Higher Education (meetings, reports, audits, ...) should be emphasized. The center should receive all the necessary information online, from the corporate network, by sending requests to the university's information system (see further in the text).

The center should hold meetings and other face-to-face gatherings only when there is a serious need, with mutual interest (ministry and university), and only with the right people.

Quality must be established through economic leverage — if a university produces high-tech companies based on innovations that are the result of its scientific research, and provides these companies with qualified personnel — this means that the university produces high-quality products and the university's activities are efficient; otherwise the university should go bankrupt. The transformation of one university unit into another, the abolition or emergence of a new unit should be reactions to the results of event analysis, marketers' forecasts and resource analysis.

The main objective of the university is to carry out startup projects, which are called "technostarting" (i.e. academic spin-off), that is, the spin-out of high-tech companies from the university [1]. Everything that happens in the university should serve this. The result of the university's activities should be personnel, science and technology (production based on innovative technology). Newly created products and services "born" by the university will subsequently be interconnected and form a set of closely cooperating and at the same time competing companies, or a cluster [2]. Clusters created in this way, later on, as a result of successful cooperation and the formation of a high-speed inter-cluster network, can even be transformed into a diversified "ecosystem" with an innovation center — the university [5].

Institute of the University — Integrator

To achieve the above goals, the university should have structures supporting solutions to these problems. Based on the experience of developed universities, it can be said that university institutes are the main structure. The institute is a university structure designed to address pressing and urgent problems focused on the competitive market. Along with teaching and research, the third main goal of the university is to develop a mechanism that brings profit to the university as a result of solving and implementing these problems. It is important that the institute not only prepares personnel for solving problems, but also solves these problems, implements solutions in life and makes a profit. The solution is the release of completely new products, the creation of new services or new industries using high technology.

The institute is engaged in solving urgent problems, and for this, the problem being solved is investigated from all sides. All of the institute's activities — education, science, technology and management — are directed toward it. For high-quality all-round inspection, analysis, design, implementation and adoption of new technologies, specialized support subdivisions are organized within the institute.

Institute subdivisions may be called departments, production centers, laboratories, programs, or otherwise, but they must be highly integrated within the framework of the institute, that is, within the framework of the problems that the institute is concerned with. The institute will solve a complex of problems from a specific problem area. The main purpose of the institute is to provide a high-quality solution to the problems posed by the institute, bringing social values. The problems (tasks) of the institute are common to all departments of the institute.

The institute periodically organizes (scientific-production) startup projects to solve the tasks assigned to the institute. The goal of the project is to qualitatively solve the problem; to produce technology (platforms, some production) as a result of solving this problem; to implement this technology in the form of specific production; to provide this production with all necessary resources, including personnel.

The project participants are students, masters, graduate students and teachers from the relevant departments of the institute. In addition, experts from relevant companies should also be included in the project, if there are any in this region, or they must work online through an information/communication network.

After the first year, every student should participate in a project, and all further activities of the student continue within this project. The student studies general education subjects, but term papers, dissertations, internships, etc. are carried out within the framework of the project. Students trained by the institute specialize in this field.

The project manager (PM) is more than a scientific supervisor. The PM is responsible for the successful completion and implementation of the project, that is, using the resources allocated for the project (people and other means) within the specified time, a high-quality innovative technology must be produced, this technology must be applied in production, the created production must be staffed, tested, implemented, and must generate profit. The PM is responsible for all of this. Trial operation of production takes place before the end of the project, and at the end of the project the newly created production is already operating legally with personnel and clients.

All scientific publications, term papers and dissertations, internships, theses, etc. are performed on the basis of research within a specific project at the institute. Each department of the institute engages in specific research within a specific project. The main point is that the problem is studied comprehensively (mathematically, economically, psychologically, linguistically, informationally, etc.) and in close cooperation between the institute's departments. This is the main difference between the institute and an ordinary department. When modeling a solution, all important characteristics are taken into account, and specialists from all interested departments of the institute (stakeholders) participate.

The institute should be in close collaboration with educational institutions and leading production companies. Cooperation with companies in this industry or in this region should be carried out in all areas of the institute's activities: education, science and innovative high-tech projects. Online cooperation with leading remote companies may be organized. A platform should be created on the institute's website for broad communication with rich capabilities for interaction with experts from relevant companies. Announcements about the institute's specific activities (webinar, online discussion, seminar, thesis defense, etc.) should not only be advertised through information/communication channels; in addition, they should also be printed in paper form and placed on the notice boards of these companies in advance. Experts from these companies should be included in the institute on the basis of cooperation. They should participate in projects, the formation of curricula and programs, at seminars and defenses of term papers and dissertations, in the organization of internships and in the conduct of some specialized courses.

Not all projects of the institute are startups, but all of them should be scientific-production projects, the results of which should be implemented in specific industries (possibly in those previously created by the institute) and bring profit to the institute. The development and prospects of the institute, however, are determined by successfully completed startup projects.

Some similar subdivisions can be created in several institutes; for example, IT subdivisions can be created in institutes for the restoration and reconstruction of historical buildings, tourism development, soil rehabilitation, etc. But these IT departments are problem-oriented and engaged in research on the application of IT in the relevant problem areas of the respective institute. The curricula of the IT departments at these institutes coincide in the first year of study and many subjects can be studied in shared groups. But in subsequent years, programs are already deeply immersed in the problems and can differ greatly from each other. Likewise, linguistic, physical, mathematical, economic and other departments can be distributed among the institutes. Of course, faculties or university-wide departments can be organized at the university for conducting general subjects for institutes.

Managing a university is inconceivable without an information system (IS), through which important life processes of the university are supported. In [20], many automated activities of the university are described, some of which are integrated, for example, with LMS, a platform for supporting educational activities. Many university IS also have a subsystem to support research activities. A modern university IS should include the main subsystem — supporting the activities of university institutes. In this subsystem, the education, science, and technology of university institutes are modeled (student training, information on a problem area, scientific achievements and problems, about startup projects, about companies, etc.). The vision of such an IS is briefly described in [21].

Institute of Systems of Informatics (ISI)

Consider the ISI. In my opinion, this institute should be a pathfinder (pioneer) in university reform. As mentioned above, IT departments can exist as subdivisions in various institutes; they are problem-oriented and highly integrated within the framework of the corresponding institute. Among the IT departments distributed across institutes are, among others, robotics, mobile devices, on-board systems, etc. Among the problem areas, there is software development (SD), that is, the development of software systems for information processing. System analysis of production processes, consulting on the reengineering of these industries, development of information systems for enterprises, automation supporting production processes, development of communication systems in the information space, development of artificial intelligence systems, development of design platforms (system programming) and application systems, etc. are the problem area of this institute. I would especially like to note that the joint areas of software development and precision mechanics, such as robotics, mobile and on-board systems, etc., which may be included in this problem area, are not considered in this context. As we can see, the ISI problem area is actually informational in nature and very relevant today. In addition, this institute is an innovation center (base) for other IT departments in the field of software development.

There are several features of this institute that enable it to be a pathfinder. As we said above, the institute solves the problem within the framework of projects, implements it in life and brings profit to the institute. As a result of the startup project, we see a new functioning profitable production. This means that the office of the enterprise, workplaces and high-quality product release must be organized. In addition, raw materials must be provided, production facilities must be provided, customers must exist and the product must be delivered to the client in a timely manner. This is the most complex set of issues that requires large resources and must be resolved within the framework of the organization of production [22].

For the problem area of ISI, this complex of problems is solved much more easily and at almost no particular cost. IT production produces digital information products. For the production of such a product, the raw material is also digital information, which is generated within a certain enterprise absolutely free of charge (unless you count electricity). For the transfer of raw materials to production and the manufactured product to the final customer (for logistics), almost nothing is required except an information network (local, industry or global). Production facilities — computers and network equipment — become cheaper year after year, and they are hardly needed for the manufacturer, because today every person has at least two computers; a student — even three (counting the educational computer).

To master the theoretical and practical foundations of solving problems in a certain field, training laboratories and other teaching aids with expensive equipment are required. Today, for many subjects at the university, appropriate computer educational systems are used to study complex phenomena and processes. In [23], the main platforms for developing virtual laboratories are listed. Virtual laboratories and other computerized teaching aids, even with rich functional capabilities [24], of course, largely support the assimilation of knowledge by students, but for research and the creation of innovative technologies, very "subtle" and still unexplored phenomena and processes are used. For this, such digital tools are not very helpful. The main reason is that the object of research is actually not digital, but real. This object is modeled and thereby abstracts from some aspects of the real object. Trained personnel cannot study the abstract properties or characteristics of an object and everything connected with them.

To study the field of software product development, the situation is fundamentally different, because the object of research is digital. This object is not modeled and does not abstract anything. The trainee works with a real object. All tools are real. For example, even for the development of robotics, an on-board or mobile software product, a software developer explores a process whose inputs and outputs are in contact (connected) with some ports of physical equipment. The behavior of this physical equipment is investigated by precision mechanics and microelectronics. A software development researcher does not go beyond these ports. But due to the fact that the developers of such software systems must closely cooperate with the developers of the physical device, appropriate stands and equipment are required; and given that the development of a physical object (precision mechanics and microelectronics) is primary in relation to software development, these areas of software development should be transferred to the IT departments of the respective institutes.

For software development, all training tools are no different from real tools. Almost all tools have community versions, open source, and can be downloaded through the Internet and used for educational purposes free of charge.

IT project management also has its own specifics. Discussion of a created or being-created artifact (document, model, module, architecture, idea, ...), semi-finished product and/or product is much simpler and more convenient compared to other types of projects. In order for the participants in a discussion to truly express their opinion about the semi-finished product or product, they should see this object; for example, for the textile industry — fiber, fabric, clothing, etc. Therefore, they must gather somewhere or receive a copy of this artifact. For software development, semi-finished products and products are digital information in the form of files; they can be easily distributed among discussion participants or posted somewhere on a common platform such as Google Drive. If the discussion is organized in any communication environment, then the location of the discussion participants themselves does not matter.

The above conclusion that the creation of an IT-based institute should be primary in modernizing or reforming higher education is not a discovery; all developed universities of the world that are considered 3rd generation universities have gone this way [3].

Examples of universities that have followed the development path that IT gave an initial impetus to are Stanford University, the Massachusetts Institute of Technology, Cambridge University and others. All these universities are innovation centers of the respective clusters geographically close to them. They are a knowledge/experience factor — one of the main factors in the development of innovative clusters [4].

Cambridge University

Consider one of them — one of the oldest and largest in the UK — Cambridge University. The role of the institute in this university is played by the so-called "schools" of the university. The school contains several faculties (a set of departments), research institutes, laboratories, etc.

At the beginning of the Cambridge cluster — the Cambridge Science and Technology Park (1970) — the employees of the cluster companies were mainly graduates of the Faculties of Mathematics and Computer Engineering of the University and former employees of the computer-aided design center [3]. The main impetus for the development of the cluster was given by Cambridge Consultants, established in 1960, which attempted to provide universities and other academic institutions with the opportunity and conditions to produce companies (in addition to training specialists and conducting science) and to provide them with new high technologies. With the help of Cambridge Consultants, over 20 companies producing IT products were created.

The name of the park indicates that the production here is high-tech, and one of the main activities of the companies was scientific research. It is for this reason, and because the university is their home, that they worked closely with the university. By 1985, the number of high-technology companies had increased to around 360. By 2000, the Cambridge Technopole had grown significantly and was home to over 1,300 technology-based firms employing around 38,000 people [Cambridge Technopole Report, 2013]. Due to the close collaboration of the Cambridge Science Park with the University of Cambridge in the field of scientific research, in addition to these new companies, large transnational high-tech companies created offices in this park. Most of these companies worked in the IT field.

By the end of the 1990s, biotechnology companies appeared in the Cambridge cluster. Further, managerial training programs appeared at the university. The university began to train personnel in the field of technostarting so that, upon the creation of new technologies, new companies would be provided with specialized personnel in this direction.

By 2013, the Cambridge Technopole had turned into a highly developed ecosystem comprising 1,525 technology-based firms employing over 53,000 people, with combined revenues of nearly £12 billion and a total market capitalisation of £50 billion [Cambridge Technopole Report, 2013].

In its industrial strategy, the UK government pays special attention to, and plans to support, those based on innovative high technologies, IT-based technology parks and everything related to them [8, 9].

Introduction of ISI to the University

As mentioned above, ISI operates with the goal of creating IT companies. An IT company will be created as a result of a startup project that is periodically organized at the institute to solve current problems in this area.

Like any company, an IT company "born" by the university is also formed on the basis of personnel trained by the university. No retraining is required for these personnel, since they "grew up" in the environment of the company's activity and the problem being solved within the framework of the startup project.

ISI, like any institute, carries out its activities (education, science, technology) based on the issues. Thus, the curriculum is also developed on the basis of the required qualifications to solve a given class of problems. The main processes in software development are:

  • IT project management
  • system analytics
  • modeling and design
  • programming in modern high-level languages
  • testing
  • maintenance

Consequently, the curriculum is developed on the basis of the above list of main processes. Due to the fact that information technologies are developing very rapidly, the process of training specialists can be modeled as an incremental (evolutionary, spiral) process. The requirements for knowledge and skill are not set by the state standard, but are developed based on the problem. In this model, the iteration does not end with university graduation, but with specialist training. As soon as a student participates in the project, feedback can be obtained — the weaknesses of preparation and the path to improvement. Based on the feedback, changes (adjustments) are developed for the primary requirements — corrective requirements. These requirements are analyzed and based on them, the curriculum and training programs are adjusted. We can say that curricula and programs are being improved on the basis of previous curricula, programs and new training requirements, that is, some increment is added. Through such consistent approximation, a specialist is prepared to solve the problem.

The innovative idea of creating an IT company begins with the development of an innovative idea for a new type of software product (SP), and the latter is based on the needs of end users of the SP. The marketing department of the institute explores the market for SP, potential competitors and their properties. Here the advice and consultations of specialists from the companies with which the institute cooperates are very important. It is also important that everyone involved in such a study first sign a non-disclosure agreement for innovative ideas. An analysis of such needs determines the availability of a market for a new type of SP and the institute's ability to organize production for the implementation of this type of SP. If possible, then the institute can take on the creation of a new company. Such a decision is made at the academic council of the institute.

Other features of the ISI (patents, business plan, business structure, etc.) are similar to those of other institutes.

Conclusion

The development of mankind and society, overcoming existing urgent problems and achieving qualitatively new heights of human life in the 21st century is unthinkable without the development of innovative high-tech industries, especially without IT production. The experience of developed societies in this regard shows that such production structures (IT parks, clusters, ecosystems, ...) should be created on the basis of a university-generator. The university should not produce "isolated" specialists and "isolated" science, but generate high-tech industries where specialists, science and innovative technology are integrated. The university should be the innovation center of this structure. Technoparks or other structures created without the participation of the university as the main innovation center are doomed to failure.
