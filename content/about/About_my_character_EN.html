About My Character

Let me dwell a little on my elementary thoughts about human psychology, which is completely unfamiliar to me. I have two purposes in writing these thoughts: 1) to understand the causes of many events that have happened to me; 2) my advice, based on my experience, may be useful to those who have such a character.

Let alone knowledge and experience of psychology, I probably could not take a place even at the edge of the circle of conversation on this subject. Respected psychologists… if, smiling at these thoughts of mine, you regard them as the views of an ordinary person based on life experience, I will be glad.

The "Mind–Heart" Criterion

Let us arrange people's characters by the criterion (scale) "brain (mind)–heart". We must never understand such an order in the set of characters as an order from positive to negative, or from good to bad. In both sets there are good and bad people. I only want to distinguish them this way: on one side of the line the factor of the brain (mind, thinking) is greater, on the other side the factor of the heart (feeling, emotion) is greater, that's all.

In psychology and physiology, in my opinion, the character of the brain (mind) is considered to be decision-making based on the left hemisphere, and correspondingly the character of the heart is considered to be decision-making based on the right hemisphere. That is, by "heart" what is actually meant is not a person's heart, but the special part of the brain that governs the factors of human character associated with the heart. On the Internet there are also several tests that determine which of the two characters mentioned above you belong to. One of them shows an animation of a swiftly running horse: if in your eyes the horse is going forward, your decision-making is based on the mind (left hemisphere); otherwise, if in your view the horse is moving backward, your decision-making is based on emotion (right hemisphere).

In my opinion, a person always acts using both hemispheres of the brain. Only when analyzing delicate, tangled (complex) matters, or carrying out such activity and making a decision on its basis, in one person the left brain is the more active, main, governing part and the right brain is the less active, auxiliary, governed part; in another person it is the opposite. Of course, there are probably also people who use both hemispheres of the brain equally.

First we define the two extreme points of this scale. There are people who are very meticulous, able to analyze a process deeply, cold-blooded (cold calculation), who think deeply in delicate situations, pay attention to details, and before starting a complex task set to work only after determining what its result will be (how expedient it is); who do not give in to feelings and act with reason. Let us conditionally call such a character the character of the "mind". There are other people who are very much given to feelings, act on intuition, have strong empathy, are sensitive to energy and mood (desire), are hot-blooded, take the complex situations of life lightly, do not sense a possible danger in advance, sometimes are unable to think at all, and who do the deed first and only after seeing its result begin to think. Let us conditionally call such a character the character of the "heart".

The characters of all people lie between these two extreme points, the characters of the mind and of the heart. People of the mind character (the factor of mind prevails over the factor of heart) speak little about their problems, successes, plans for the future. Their mental state is also not visible on their faces. They do not trust quickly, are not deceived quickly, and neither take offense nor rejoice quickly. With people of the heart character, it is enough to talk for 10–15 minutes and you will learn everything about them: an open book. Their emotional, mental states are evident from their faces.

Life educates a person throughout his life. A person learns something for himself from every day, event, conversation, occurrence, situation. People of the mind character use this knowledge gained from life expediently. By "expediently" I mean: they learn, study and draw conclusions about a needed element (behavior, action, attitude, activity, …) which lies on the path of the set goal and which they had not known until then, and, most importantly, they are able to apply it in their own conduct. An element they consider positive (needed, expedient) they can add to their conduct even if this element does not please them, and a negative one they can remove from their conduct even if they like this element very much. As a result, a positive change toward the goal they have in view occurs in their conduct (life). People of the heart character, on the contrary, use this knowledge gained from life according to desire. An element they like (that matches their desire) they add to their conduct even if it does not match the goal (plan) they set for themselves, and one they do not like they remove from their conduct even if they consider it expedient (supporting their plan). As a result, a positive change occurs in their mood, their mental state.

Both characters must have a certain degree of will in introducing a new element into their life or removing it from their life. People of the mind character achieve their goals in life in most cases, whereas people of the heart character more often consider themselves happy in life.

The opinion that people of the mind character have no feelings and people of the heart character have no mind is completely wrong. The main point is which factor prevails. The feelings, emotion, empathy (desire) of people of the mind character are governed by their "mind", and the "mind" of people of the heart character is governed by their desire. For example, if a person of the mind character must feel some event from the heart, that is, if his mind commands him that it is necessary to feel this event, he can feel it; he too has feelings, only they are governed by the mind, that is, mind and goal are primary, feeling (desire) is secondary, governed. In the same way, if a person of the heart character wants to think deeply about some event, he can think deeply and draw a logical conclusion; he too has a mind, only it is governed by feeling (desire), that is, he uses his mind only if he wants to: desire is primary, governing, and mind (thinking) is secondary, governed.

There are events that will certainly move any person of the mind character regardless of his thoughts (make him laugh, delight him, make him cry, make him pity, …), and there are events that force any person of the heart character, regardless of his desire, to think, to reflect. But such events are very rare.

Let me try to explain with an example. Suppose a person in the course of his work encountered a certain manager. In the work process he talks with the manager often and comes to know that he does not like the manager's character. But the work itself suits him, and the monthly salary satisfies him. A person of the mind character, having determined how to relate to the manager, can calmly work under this manager; but a person of the heart character cannot: he will leave the job.

My Character

With this I think I have described my character sufficiently. My character is the character of the heart. This character of mine played the main role in many events that happened to me.

My conduct, actions, and attitude toward life and toward others were based mainly on feeling, emotion, (emotional) empathy. On the basis of the development of these aspects, my conduct, actions, and attitude toward life and others developed. Of course, I too, like others, learned and understood something from every day, event, conversation, occurrence, situation, but… I could not apply it in my life. I tried, but I could not manage. Each mistake I made was repeated again and again, without any change. The proverb "A blind man loses his stick only once" seems not to be for me.

I still remember the words I said at the 30-year reunion of classmates: "We will not make any mistakes now, we will measure seven times and cut once…" Ha-ha-ha… You won't make mistakes…? I said those words then as if I had fully drawn conclusions from my life full of mistakes, had fully learned life, and knew exactly what to do, how to live, what to beware of, and what to be guided by.

I thought that all my classmates, like me, know: we walked the uneven, up-and-down road of life and learned how to walk this road, and now we would apply only this knowledge, skill, experience, and habits in our lives. No, I was mistaken. In the delicate situations of life, at times when decisions had to be made on the basis of mind and logic, I erred again and again.

A close friend (classmate) of mine, whenever he sees me, scolds me: "You're still the same hasty one, you first do a thing, then think, hasty one, fool, your character won't change till you die." My friend speaks 100% truth, and I am not offended by him at all.

Of course, I observe, as far as I can, the universal rules of order, the laws of the state, our culture, and customs. But a person in his life encounters such delicate situations in which how you conduct yourself, your behavior, depends sharply on your character. In such situations there are usually two paths. The first path: to analyze the situation deeply, pay attention to all its delicate sides, seek a solution on the basis of the analysis result, relying on logic, think about the solution options and what they lead to, and make a decision on that basis. The second path: a voice coming from the heart, a path (command) given by the heart. No logic works, you do not think about the result, the option is one. In me, most often it is this second path that works. Sometimes the result of what is done by this path may not agree even with your conscience; having done the deed, I suffer for a long time, but when such a situation occurs again, I repeat the same mistake again.

Luckily, my children's upbringing was mainly handled by my wife. Otherwise, God forbid, what would have become of my sons' upbringing. Still, in my eldest son too the elements of haste, hot temper, and not thinking about the end are not absent; they must have passed on with the blood. Now just think how my life partner has lived with me for more than 40 years. Another woman in her place would long ago have divorced me. I thank God a thousand times that He sent me such a patient woman. She probably said: "Let this child live too." May God give my life partner patience to live with me until the end of my days!

Positive Traits of People of the Heart Character

People of the heart character have positive traits too. For the most part they engage in the activity they themselves want; for them the process of the activity itself is more important than its result. For example, when playing football the score is not important, the game itself is. In my youth, in football games I was mostly the goalkeeper. When our defenders were repelling the ball, I would wait: "if only the opposing player would get past our defender and kick toward my goal, would I be able to save it?" or "if only our defender would trip the opponent and an 11-meter penalty kick would be awarded against our goal." I wanted not to stand in the goal for nothing, but to join the game.

If you want to know a person who is glad about his own mistake, that is a person of the heart character, because if he makes a mistake, the mistake must be corrected, for this the activity must be carried out again, and for a person of the heart character this is good: instead of finishing the activity he likes, he continues it.

Of course, every process, however short, has its own cycle (period); that is, the result of one task is the aggregation of the results of small tasks (actions). For people of the heart character these small results are important; the overall result, the benefit of the activity, is not important. For example, while mowing grass, if the grass keeps falling from the hand; while catching fish, if the fish keeps escaping from the hook; while analyzing some problem, if it cannot be decomposed or abstracted… a person of the heart character does not want this. But he can easily leave the mown grass lying in the field, throw the caught fish back into the irrigation ditch; the grade for the knowledge he has gained is not important.

At school, at university, in postgraduate study, in professional development courses, grades had no importance for me at all; rather, the process of learning, knowledge, was important. When talking with someone, the result of the conversation is not important; the meaning in the conversation, the depth of thoughts, the logic and beauty are. It is like dancing, listening to music, watching an artist's work. For people of the heart character the present moment is more important than tomorrow.

Now I want to dwell on how my life, my working life after my student years, went with this character.

Republican Main Computing Center, Bukhara Branch

When I was studying at the university, for some reason scientific work seemed not to be for me: you get up in the morning, go to work, work is over and you forget everything about work, go home and are busy with your family, on Saturday and Sunday you go somewhere with your family, on your work vacation you go on a trip. In Yevgeny Leonov's words (from "Gentlemen of Fortune"), "boredom" was for me a very strong desire. This was engineering work. Despite having ability and opportunity for the scientific field, I chose engineering work and gave up scientific work.

At the Republican Main Computing Center, Bukhara branch, I started work as an engineer-programmer. I liked my job very much: the courtyard of our institution, the machine hall, the room assigned to me, the girl operators drew me in. I met my love right there, among the girl operators; this doubled my heart's desire for the workplace and for my profession.

The head of the department was Ravshan aka Sharipov, a graduate of our own school, my father's pupil and my mentor; he was a mentor to me both in the profession and in life. With Ravshan aka we spent many wonderful nights in the machine hall with the Minsk-32 and ES-1022 computers, carrying them on until dawn. I began to feel like a professional programmer; computer software enchanted me; now what drew me was not writing programs for ordinary tasks but going deeper inside, watching the beauty there and taking pleasure from it.

One invitation for professional development came to our institution from the Central Asian Scientific Training Center (STC) located in Tashkent. It was necessary to study for three months in Tashkent on the Assembler programming language, take an exam, and obtain a certificate. This professional development had no effect on salary or career: you simply go, study, get the certificate and return, and that's it. None of my colleagues wished to go, but for me this was a demand of the heart. I immediately said to Ravshan aka: "I will go." For the institution too it was awkward to report "upstairs" that "we have no volunteers," and this choice of mine pleased the institution as well, and they immediately sent me on the training trip.

There… again Tashkent, again the same streets, younger brothers, again the same recklessness….

One thing amazed me. The STC was the only one in Central Asia; prominent research staff of the former Union in the field of programming, professors and scholars, taught there, and the students too were from all the republics of the former Union: from Moscow, Leningrad, Minsk, from the Baltic, Caucasus, and Central Asian republics. It follows that this center was one of the strong scientific training centers of the former Union that could be counted on the fingers.

The classes were very interesting and fascinating. I understood clearly that the scientific field of software engineering was mine, the desire of my heart, and that conducting research in this field was a dream of mine that I could not give up.

My duties at the center, the scientific atmosphere there, debates with fellow students, and experiments on the computer outweighed the nights, pranks, and recklessness I had planned with my younger brothers. I did not notice how the three months passed; having passed the exams, received the certificate, and said goodbye to the center and new friends, I returned to Bukhara.

Two years later I took the next similar professional development, a course on OS generation for the ES computers' OS. These two courses meant for me that I should begin to engage fully in scientific work.

Later, in the 2000s, when I went to work in Tashkent, one day I wanted to see that very STC to which I had grown attached, and I went… An abandoned building, some windows broken, it had turned into a very pitiable place… My heart ached, I was very upset, I regretted it: what a pity, that era…, that building…, that environment…

During the time I was working at the computing center an ugly incident occurred. In those days, in the mid-1980s, frequent changes of leaders of the former Union and the Republic, the first sprouts of economic difficulties, and other events led at enterprises to a relative decline in work activity, to some employees turning their attention from direct work to other negative sides of the enterprise, and to the emergence of a mood of discontent. Our enterprise, of course, was not free of shortcomings either, but our collective was harmonious, and we were satisfied with our work and our pay.

One of those days our Mahmud Boronov aka (a course-mate of my sister Mayram, nephew of a journalist of all-Union rank named Yodosh Muqimov, a close friend of Ravshan aka), in a circle of close friends (4–5 people), spoke about negative events at the enterprise, about people who are on the list but absent from work, and said that these shortcomings must be reported to the higher-ups. He also said that these claims were confirmed by facts. We, of course, expressed agreement with this opinion. Within a few days a letter was drawn up, and we signed our names under it.

Here a decision should have been made on the basis of the mind. I should have answered fairly, with my mind, questions such as: "Are the facts confirmed by documents?", "Did you see them with your own eyes?", "Do these shortcomings interfere with your personal work?", "What is the attitude of the enterprise's leadership toward you?", "Are there such shortcomings in other enterprises too?" and so on. The answer to each question, given by the true mind, would not have allowed me to sign such a letter. But my heart said to me: "You are in a team, never damage friendship, respect your elder brothers, their opinions, their decisions," and I did so.

A commission came, conducted an inspection, and found the "facts" in our letter incorrect. An enterprise meeting was held, and the commission announced that the facts in our letter were false. Several of our colleagues spoke and criticized us. The head of the enterprise, may God have mercy on him, Hakim aka Sharipov, spoke last and said: "The boys acted out of youth, let us all together forgive them, and let us work as brothers as if nothing had happened." This very leadership, a little (a few months) before, had given me a two-room apartment on behalf of the enterprise. It seemed to me as if everyone was looking at me and saying "ungrateful." If only the earth would split and I could sink into it…

In those days a joyful event also occurred: our first child Rustamjon was born. It was 2–3 days after that meeting; one day when I was at work, at about 10 in the morning I was told that there was a call for me from the enterprise reception, and I went and picked up the receiver. On the other end was my wife, Maqsuda. I never told her about the events that took place at work.

Maqsuda told me that they had been discharged from the maternity hospital and that I should quickly take a taxi, go, pick them up from the maternity hospital and bring them to Khargush. I told her that I had no way to, that the enterprise would not release me.

Hakim Sharipovich (the director of the enterprise) had a habit. Sometimes he would pick up the receiver of the telephone on his desk and secretly listen to what others were saying. During Maqsuda's conversation with me, he said over the telephone: "Telman, come to me quickly." I put down the receiver and went in to the director. Hakim aka's face was entirely different, anger was boiling in it. I understood and thought that it must be about our complaint letter.

Hakim aka: "Telman, after all, we are human beings, aren't we?"
I: "Yes, Hakim Sharipovich."
Hakim aka: "I am a human being, why do you think this way about me, Telman?"
I: "I have no bad opinion of you at all right now."
Hakim aka: "Then why did you say that to the daughter-in-law?"
I: "After all, it is working time, my machine time is near, how can I go?"
Hakim aka: "Oh, to hell with that work, and the machine, and the machine time! Please, don't think of me that way. Go right now, take the daughter-in-law and your little son from the hospital, run, drop all your work, I will arrange your machine time myself," he said.

I truly realized that in life I do not understand many things, that I do not know where a person's humanity shows, what a red line is for a person.

Years later my younger sister Zuhro took, for her son, a daughter-in-law from that very village of Hakim aka in G'ijduvon. From the in-laws I learned that Hakim aka would come to the wedding. At the wedding I wanted to apologize for the mistake I had made many years before, and went up to him. We chatted with pleasure. Just think: we recalled only good things; my foolishness did not come to our minds… The apology slipped my mind. Later, as I learned, Hakim aka passed away. Hakim aka turned out to be one of the true human beings who worked as leaders in those very mid-1980s, and my conscience often reminds me of this…

After that ugly incident, it was the same heart of mine, the one that had told me to sign the letter, that told me I had to leave the enterprise, and I left my job. Yes…, having tasted the salt of the enterprise that had done me so much good, gave me my love, gave me an apartment, enchanted my heart anew with science, and spitting into its salt shaker, I left, showing extreme disrespect to such a sacred place. To this day my conscience torments me…

In my heart there was still an ember of engaging in science. I wanted to go and get a job at the Bukhara Pedagogical Institute; they told me the engineer-programmer position would become vacant after a year, and I needed to work somewhere for a year.

I wanted to get a job at the planning and economics technical school; they agreed to hire me, only the deputy director for academic affairs told me to write a lesson plan, that he would check my plan and then admit me to teach. After all, it is so everywhere, but not for me; I did not like this and did not go to the job.

I went to get a job at the teachers' professional development institute. The institute had a display classroom, and the engineer-programmer position turned out to be vacant. The head of the department told me I could start work from tomorrow, and even gave me the key. Happy, I came to work in the morning and began work in the display classroom. A little later the telephone rang; I picked up the receiver and it was the head of the department. He told me that before he arrived I should wipe his desk and brew tea, and I said to him in reply: "Be well, I am leaving, the key is with the watchman."

As a result I got a job as a computer science teacher at the 34th vocational college (bilim yurti) near Chor-Minor. After a year of teaching, rich in interesting events, I got a job as an engineer-programmer in the display classroom of the Bukhara Pedagogical Institute.

Display Classroom of the Bukhara Pedagogical Institute

My main goal was to get into some scientific school in the IT field. In my heart the flame of the impressions I had received from the training center in Tashkent had not yet died out.

The display classroom belonged to the Department of Computational Mathematics, and the head of the department was Toshpo'lot aka Sharipov. I went in to see him and explained my goal. Toshpo'lot aka fully supported my idea and said that our department really did need a specialist in IT with an academic degree and that my plan was just what was needed; he went in to the scientific secretary of the institute and asked him to allocate one postgraduate place for the department. The queue number was 180th. For me the academic degree was not the main thing, it had no importance at all. The main thing was to become acquainted with the inner subtleties of programming, to "watch" it and take pleasure in it.

In the department, Isomiddin aka Jalolov had recently returned from Novosibirsk after defending his candidate's dissertation and was an assistant to Toshpo'lot aka. In the 180th queue one would have had to wait 5–6 years, but what Toshpo'lot aka and Isomiddin aka, both of them, did, I do not know: within one year they obtained for me a referral to Novosibirsk for a 6-month internship and then to postgraduate study.

But at the institute the atmosphere was different, I did not like it. Everyone addressed one another according to academic degree, according to position; for an ordinary engineer without an academic degree like me it was hard to turn to some department and get a positive result; there was not even a "whiff" here of the atmosphere of the scientific training center in Tashkent. But the collective of our department was different, their respect toward me was high; after all, they did me a kindness they had not done for their own relatives.

At the end of 1987 my mood was excellent, because in 1 week I was expected to go, on a referral, to Novosibirsk, one of the strongest scientific centers in the world. Having celebrated the New Year holiday, I flew to Novosibirsk.

Novosibirsk, Akademgorodok, Computing Center

Novosibirsk, Akademgorodok, everything around is snow-white; it turns out the winter in Siberia is very beautiful.

In the 8th dormitory Hakim aka (may God have mercy on him) was waiting for me. Hakim aka Akhmedov taught at our faculty, in the Department of Mathematical Analysis. Soon he was to defend his candidate's dissertation. That is why he had come to Akademgorodok for a professional development course of 1 or 2 months.

Hakim aka introduced me to Akademgorodok and to Shuhrat aka from Bukhara. Shuhrat aka Hodiyev, originally from Shofirkon, after finishing his studies in Tashkent, was doing scientific work in Novosibirsk, at the Computing Center. Isomiddin aka had obtained the consent of Shuhrat aka's scientific supervisor, Igor Vasilyevich Pottosin, to be my scientific supervisor as well.

I met Shuhrat aka, and he introduced me to our scientific laboratory. Pottosin, it turned out, came later. They told me that he would be here any moment, that I must certainly meet him, tell him that I had arrived, and receive my first task. A long corridor ran past the door of our laboratory, and at the end of the corridor, it turned out, was Pottosin's office. I went out to the door and waited for Pottosin. After a short while a man, stepping slowly, reached the end of the corridor, opened the door of that same office of Pottosin and went in. Passing by me he said "Good morning," and I too said "Good morning."

I have absolutely no interest in how people dress, and I myself am not very interested in dressing with taste, but the clothes that man wore drew my attention. A yellow, quite dirty raincoat, two or three of its buttons, I think, torn off, threads hanging. Boots on his feet, the laces of both untied. On his head an ushanka hat, just like the postman Pechkin, with one ear flap down… I thought that he was probably a cleaner or a watchman, but even cleaners and watchmen probably do not dress so unremarkably.

The door of our laboratory opened, Shuhrat aka came out and asked me: "Hasn't Pottosin come?" I said: "No, I didn't see him pass." Shuhrat aka said: "Why is he late, he was supposed to come," and went toward Pottosin's door. Opening the door, he closed it again and said to me: "Why, he has come, he's right there!" I was astonished: could that man who passed be the head of our laboratory, whom mature scholars of the IT field around the world know? "Shall we go together, I will introduce you," said Shuhrat aka. I said: "No, I'll go myself" and headed toward Pottosin's office. I opened the door: a small corridor, on the left a door open, from the door clouds of cigarette smoke were pouring. Looking in through the door, I saw Pottosin puffing out clouds of smoke, smoking a cigarette and writing something. I said: "Hello," and he, looking at me, said: "Hello." I said: "I'm Telman, I've come from Bukhara." Then he got up from his place at once and, delighted, shook hands with me. He lacked either one or two teeth, which looked very strange.

He asked about Bukhara, about my family, about how Toshpo'lot aka and Isomiddin aka were doing. We talked for a long time, and at the end of the conversation he told me which tasks to perform, their deadlines, and which university courses I should attend.

At Novosibirsk State University I came across a wonderful custom. First-year and second-year students come in the morning, take a seat in the library, and read what they have planned themselves. Whichever class they need to go to, they go; if they do not want to, they do not. No roll call is taken. After class they again return to the library, sit in the same places, and continue their studies. The special courses assigned to me by Pottosin are for those who wish. Those who wish gather in the auditorium, the teacher comes and agrees on the class time with the students. The time can usually be set at any hour in the interval from 15:00 to 23:00. That is, at the university life boils until midnight. I liked this custom very much.

Students of the upper (3rd–5th) years go in the morning to a research institute and begin their work in a laboratory. Whichever class they want to attend, they go to the university and come back. All (dozens of) research institutes are located around the university, about 5–10 minutes' walk from it.

In our laboratory a Russian young man named Timofey, who studied in the 3rd year of the university, also worked. I agreed with him to attend the special courses together. Timofey and I went to the 1st class, and the lecturer began his lecture. The course was devoted to theoretical programming. However hard I tried, I understood nothing. It was not about the Russian language: I understood Russian well enough, but I did not understand the content of the lecture. I looked beside me, and Timofey was busy drawing a picture of a duck in his notebook. I was surprised and thought: this must be a very difficult subject, and Timofey too attended this class just for show, and, distracting himself, sits drawing. Suddenly Timofey says: "Wait, Semyon Viktorovich, I think there is an inaccuracy there. If one bears in mind the previous conditions, then your specific conclusion is incorrect, it should be like this," just like that corrected the lecturer, can you imagine…. The lecturer: "… Yes, Tima, perhaps you're right…" and corrected his mistake.

I was astonished. A 3rd-year student, glancing at the board only now and then and occupied with something else entirely, all of a sudden corrects a professor's mistake, while I, a university graduate, a future postgraduate student who had been directly engaged in programming for several years, listen to the lecture with my whole being and understand nothing… My heart skipped a beat. It told me: "Don't rate yourself highly," and my mind said: "Yes, you are not yet ready for such a lecture."

The next day I went in to see Pottosin, I was very upset. I explained what had happened and said: "I'm probably going back to Bukhara." Pottosin first put down his cigarette and laughed heartily, and then said to me: "Telman, first of all, I have already tested you, you really are a gifted person, no compliments. Please don't compare yourself with Timofey, he is a prodigy, they wrote compilers already in the 9th grade of school, for the last 2 years of school he studied at Stanford, we have many such, God give them health." I came around considerably. For the first time I found out that in the field of programming I was still a "little child."

After that I began to learn what real programming is, its magic, its mysteries and secrets, and at the same time to enjoy the enchantment of programming. Having passed through the periods of internship and postgraduate study, rich in adventures, and having defended my candidate's dissertation, I returned to Bukhara.

Computing Center of Bukhara State University

When I returned from Novosibirsk, the academic council of the university, on Toshpo'lot aka's recommendation, elected me head of the university's computing center. Since I was the only certified specialist in IT (information technologies) at the university, the leadership listened to my opinion. At about the same time as me, my close friend from my student days, Rahmatullo, also came to work at the university. Since he was a doctor of sciences, he was appointed head of the Department of Informatics and Information Technologies.

Although I had classes in the department, my main task was to analyze the information processes taking place at the university and to optimize them through automation. With Rahmatullo I worked very well for 1–2 years. In the course of work I got to know Rahmatullo's character well: his character was the character of the mind. He worked only for the sake of the goal. Rahmatullo's character got along very well with the character of my friend Tolib Sohibov, the son of my maternal aunt, my classmate.

The Central Bank hired us (Rahmatullo, Tolib, and me) for 1 year for a project on automating Uzbekistan's banking system; Ravshan aka Sharipov was the project head. During this 1 year we worked on one project and got to know each other even better. My heart character and the mind character of Rahmatullo and Tolib: we could not come to agreement at all. An hour of conversation was enough and we would, of course, start arguing, but if I did not see them for 3 days, I would miss them. For some reasons the project did not end successfully, and Rahmatullo and I returned to the university.

After some time Rahmatullo was appointed vice-rector for IT. That is, Rahmatullo became my direct superior. Work on automating the information processes at the university had to be carried out. I had my own plan. Rahmatullo had his own plan. Our plans differed from each other. Each of us formed our own team, and Rahmatullo invited Tolib to his team as well. We started the work. The plan had to be defended before the rector. Rahmatullo won. That is, the rector said to me: "You will work on the basis of Rahmatullo's plan, under Rahmatullo's leadership." My mind said: "Work," and my heart said: "Leave." After 1 week I wrote a resignation letter. Most of the staff of the computing center also left for some reason.

The IT Company "Intelligent Solution"

I had two professions: the first was teaching, I loved my subject very much; the second was programmer (dasturchi) in the broad sense. I loved this profession very much too. By the decree of fate I worked in both fields in turn. 1981–1986: programmer; 1986–1988: teacher; 1988–1994: intern researcher and postgraduate student; 1994–1997: teacher-programmer; 1997–1999: programmer; 1999–2003: teacher-programmer; 2004–2009: programmer; 2009–2022: teacher.

My close pupil and friend Odil Abdullayev worked as a programmer at an IT company in Tashkent. Just think: 1–2 days after I resigned from the university, the general manager of this company, Bikesh Kurmangaliyeva, called me and invited me to work.

The job was project manager; the main client was the Ministry of Social Protection of Kazakhstan. I often had to travel to Astana and back. The project manager's main duty is working with the client: creating a software system, based on his requirements, together with the members of one's project team, handing it over to the client, and having him sign the act confirming that the work has been handed over. The general manager dealt with money matters.

The project manager's duty was not mine, that is, I did not like this work and I could not do it. I found another way: I took on part of the project members' duties; we agreed that systems analyst and system designer was my job, and writing code and testing were the duty of the other team members. There were 4 project participants: a Jew, a Ukrainian, a Korean, and a Kazakh. I worked with the client too, but I worked nominally; the general manager would come to my help and resolve the main conflict and risky issues herself. Bikesh was a wonderful woman; besides being a very strong manager, she was a true human being, and she spared neither material nor moral help for me. Until 2009 I worked with pleasure. I spent most of my time in Astana.

When I came to Bukhara in 2006 for the classmates' reunion, I stayed a day or two at the house in the village. My late father said to me: "We have grown old, when will you come?" I said: "Father, give me 1 more year of time, I will put my affairs in order a little." A year later, when I was at the Tashkent house, my father and my late mother came to visit us, and then my father said: "You said 1 year…" I said: "Father, 1 more year, and that's all." A year later the same question again; I said: "Father, 1 more year." My father said: "If you don't come by the end of 2008, there will be no need for you to come after that." These words were a red line for me. My father and mother had truly grown old, and did not want to be left dependent on their sons-in-law. Now the decision had to be made not on the basis of the heart but on the basis of the mind.

Saying goodbye to Bikesh, who had helped me a great deal, to my very harmonious colleagues, to everyone in our Tashkent office, I returned to Bukhara. Yes, here, whatever my heart (desire) commanded, I submitted 100% to my mind, and this decision saved me from living my whole life in the torment of conscience. With my father and mother we lived together for three and a half years. I taught at the university.

Teaching Activity

In our (Uzbekistan's) higher education, a student's duty is similar to a schoolchild's: a student must attend classes; if he enters a class late, he must ask the teacher's permission to enter the auditorium; if the need arises to step out during class, he must ask the teacher's leave and go out only if permitted. But in my first class I would say: "If you wish, come to class; if you don't wish, don't come; it is enough that you pass the exam. If you come late, quietly go and take your seat, there is no need to ask my permission. If you want to go out, quietly go out, there is no need to ask permission." These words of mine reached the dean's office, how many times I heard remarks, I even received verbal reprimands, but it was of no use.

Checking written work was a very hard task for me, and I do not like this work. First, when written work is being done, it must be ensured that students do not use extra resources (crib sheets, computer, mobile communication, …). Second, one has to check text written by a student in "beautiful handwriting." Even leaving aside that students may help one another and other circumstances, this work was for me a disliked task. In my first lecture I conveyed the following "rules" of mine to the students:

  • If any student finds 1 mistake in my lecture, I guarantee the grade "4" (good); if he finds 2 or more mistakes, the grade "5" (excellent);
  • At the beginning of the lecture I ask 2–3 questions and assess the answers. After my last lecture I analyze the answers and grade accordingly;
  • If any student does not want to listen to my lecture, I ask him not to enter my class. If he does enter, let him occupy himself with some activity so as not to disturb us (the class).

For me it was very easy, because such a class process pleased me very much. Debating with students about the topic being studied is very much to my liking; a student's critical view of my opinion and his expressing his own opinion is a pleasure for me; if he found my mistake, I would become close friends with that student.

I did collect written work, but I did not sit and check written work; I put in my own grade. During written work I did not exercise supervision either. About 15–20 percent of students took part in the question-and-answer at the beginning of the lecture. 2–3% of students received "excellent," 4–5% "good," and 9–12% "satisfactory." I worked with these students; as for the rest, I did not bother with them. But such pedagogical activity absolutely did not conform to the rules of our higher education.

I repeated my own way every year, even though it was a mistaken way. If you analyze it well, my way is based not on the mind but on the heart! I wanted the student to converse with me, find my mistake, not be afraid of me, "open up." If some student tried but could not manage, even if his answer was wrong, I would give him a high grade. This, if you pay attention, is not motivation. In grading a child, not in the process of upbringing, not at the time of imparting knowledge, but precisely at the moment of assessing his knowledge, NEVER follow your heart. But I could not abide by this golden rule.

The Department

In 2012, our dean Shavkat aka Mirzayev, who had taken part in my father's funeral rites, said to me: "Now we are handing the department over to you." When, after the mourning rites were over, I went to work, against my own wish, the dean Shavkat, on his own part, made a proposal to the university's academic council, and I was elected head of the "Information Technologies" department. That I did not want to work in this position is a second matter; the main matter is that I could not cope with this position. More precisely, I could not cope in our university environment.

According to our university's statute, the main duties of the head of the department were as follows:

  • developing the scientific potential of the teachers;
  • developing scientific and methodological ties with other higher educational institutions and abroad;
  • improving the quality of the teaching process and the quality of students' knowledge.

But at the university other factors operated, which did not suit my character at all:

  • monitoring the timely preparation of teachers' educational-methodological packages;
  • monitoring the process of preparing translations of textbooks into the Uzbek language;
  • monitoring the writing of textbooks in the Uzbek language;
  • timely preparation of teachers' teaching loads and class distribution;
  • monitoring the timely preparation by teachers of course programs and plans for subjects;
  • regularly monitoring that teachers have no utility debts;
  • monitoring the employment of graduating students;
  • …

The duties of a head of department as I conceived them and the duties listed above did not intersect with each other at all. I have set out my thoughts about the university's activity in my scientific articles titled "Information Technologies in the Development of the University" and "Problems of Our Higher Education."

Every day there were meetings, and the participation of the head of the department in the meeting was mandatory. I usually did not attend meetings. Before a meeting Shavkat aka would say: "Today take part, delicate issues will be considered," and I would say: "Aka Shavkat, let me go, a task has come up for me, I can't." Then Shavkat aka, saying "Go, don't show yourself to my eyes," would take offense. But the next day we were like brothers, as if nothing had happened. Shavkat aka, despite all my shortcomings, is an open-hearted, conscientious man. To put it in Russian: "I would go on a scouting mission with this man without hesitation."

The PROMIS Project

On the basis of the European Union's Tempus program, the PROMIS project was organized in 2013. It had 2 groups of participants: 1 — European universities: from France, Germany, Poland, and Finland; 2 — Central Asian universities. A competition was announced, in the competition our university won and became a participant in the project. Universities from Uzbekistan (Tashkent, Bukhara), Tajikistan (Dushanbe, Khujand), Kazakhstan (Astana, Almaty), Kyrgyzstan (Bishkek, Osh), and Turkmenistan (Ashgabat) took part.

We got acquainted with European scholars, went to European countries, took part in trainings, and considerably improved our qualifications. In the course of the project I became convinced of one thing. European scholars love their profession very much and work from the heart. Not for money, there is desire. I am not saying that they were of the heart character: among them too there are people similar to me and people of the mind character, but in choosing a profession, all without exception are based not on the mind but on the heart. For them, not career, academic degree, or money, but the very engagement in science, the very process of teaching, the very processes of analyzing a problem are important, and give them delight.

Even after the project ended, these scholars came several times to our university and organized seminars for students, absolutely free of charge. To this day my contact with them has not been broken, and on holidays we congratulate one another, noble people!

Here I consider it proper to tell about one incident. The PROMIS project envisaged opening a new IT master's program. For some reason our ministry did not give permission for such a master's program. I, as coordinator of the Bukhara team, warned the general manager, the Frenchman Jean Michel, about this. Jean Michel said to me: "My assistant and I will go to Tashkent, you come too, we will go in to the minister and explain."

Our Tashkent coordinator went to the ministry and signed up for the queue. Having found out when the turn would come, he informed us. On the appointed day I went to Tashkent. The French also flew in. 1 hour before the appointed time we went to the ministry and waited for the reception. The time came, but the minister was not there. After 1 hour he came; in his reception room, besides us, there was 1 more person, who had signed up after us. A representative of the reception room said that we still had to wait. I felt very awkward. After all, for the development of our higher education, scholars had come specially from France, had come before time, had waited 2 hours in the reception room, and the minister was making them wait still more…. I was ashamed not for myself but for my Motherland. After we had waited 3 hours in the reception room, a representative came out and said that the minister would not be able to receive us, and that if we wished we could go in to the 2nd deputy, can you imagine… The earth did not split, I did not sink into the ground.

Retirement

After this incident I was completely disillusioned with higher education. The PROMIS project ended successfully in 2017. My duty as head of the department also ended. Now I was an ordinary docent and met with the teachers of the department only now and then; the rest of the time it was the auditorium, the students, my beloved profession.

In 2019 I retired and moved to a half-time position, because every year I had to go to my children in London. And in 2022, saying goodbye to everyone, I ended my working life.

Conclusion

There, my friend, is the influence of my character on my life. Yes, I made very many mistakes, and what's more, repeated mistakes, and most importantly, I hurt many…, offended…, gave pain to the hearts of…. But one thing I know for certain: I did these things without thinking about the end, and I never had the intention of offending anyone. All the blame lies in my making decisions while giving in to the heart. Yes, I could not achieve much in life, because I did not move toward a goal at all; for me there was neither yesterday nor tomorrow, I lived with today, with my heart's present desire. I simply had no goal. This brought me to today. I am pleased with my present state, may the evil eye spare me, I am very happy. Only now and then my conscience troubles me, that's all!
