A Gift to the School
A childhood memory from 1963–1964

It was the 1960s, around 1963–1964. Our house was in the center of Khargush village, right by the paved road that connected the eastern villages (like Kunjak, Khayrobodcha, etc.) with the ancient city of Bukhara. From our village to the Bukhara—Tashkent highway to the west was only 4 kilometers, and from there north was about 15-16 kilometers, which took you straight into the embrace of the city of Bukhara.

Right across from our house, on the other side of the paved road, was the Khargush middle school. From our courtyard gate to the school threshold was only 100-150 meters. However, my kindergarten was to the west, about 500-600 meters away. We went to the kindergarten not by the main road, but behind the houses, through narrow paths between private garden plots. The kindergarten building was a relatively ancient and historic structure, perhaps 80 or 100 years old...

The kindergarten was a grand single-story building made of baked bricks, with walls plastered with white ganch. This building stood out from others in the village with its wide corridor and two large rooms. The corridor was not just a passage, but the hub of our childhood life: in one corner hung our clothes, and in another, potties were neatly lined up. But the most interesting part of this corridor was a huge piano that stood heavy and mysterious in a corner.

The kindergarten gate opened to the east. On the north side of the building was a vast orchard with massive shade trees that seemed to create a green roof over our heads. Their shade was so thick that sunlight barely reached the ground, and in the summer heat, it was always cool and pleasant there.

Lunch in the shaded orchard. I am at the left table, yellow shirt. 1963–1964.

In this historic photograph, kept as a souvenir of those unforgettable days, the moment of our lunch in that very shaded orchard is captured. We, the happy children of that era, are sitting around simple wooden tables. Our caregivers, in snow-white uniforms and blue scarves, are serving us. Here, not only our bodies but also our souls were nourished with love and sincerity...

After lunch, the sleep bell would ring, and all children were called to the mandatory daytime nap. This was an unwritten but strict law of the kindergarten. Even if you didn't feel sleepy, you had to lie in your bed and close your eyes. The caregivers watched us closely. If anyone 'broke the law' — a severe punishment awaited them: they were made to stand in the corner for 10-15 minutes. Only one excuse could lead us out of that silent bedroom — the need to go to the bathroom in that large corridor...

I had planned to escape from the kindergarten many times but didn't know when or how to do it. That day, I didn't have an escape plan in mind either — only a real need led me to the corridor. Everyone was in a deep sleep. The caregivers were busy whispering among themselves.

I went out into the corridor with permission. At that same moment, my classmate Salohiddin appeared for the same reason. We were both sitting next to each other on two potties. Suddenly, my eyes fell on the entrance door, which was slightly ajar. At that very fateful second, the escape plan came back to life in my head. I looked at Salohiddin and whispered quietly:

— Do you want to escape?
"Do you want to escape?" — the fateful moment in the kindergarten corridor
— Where to? — Salohiddin asked with surprise.
— We're going to the school! — I answered with full confidence. — We'll watch the grown-ups' lessons there.
— Do you know the way? — he asked again with a bit of hesitation.
— Of course I know! We'll go by the main road; we won't get lost.
— Okay, let's go!

To keep the caregivers from noticing where we had vanished, we made a 'strategic' decision: we took the potties with us! Thus, we two mischievous children, potties in hand, quietly walked out of the slightly open door of the kindergarten. The midday sun was beating down mercilessly, and there wasn't a soul on the street. We turned right and, with slow but steady steps, headed toward the school, toward the 'world of adults'...

After walking a bit, we reached the central paved highway leading straight to Bukhara and turned east. The surrounding scenery was familiar to us: on the left was the Khargush clinic, and on the right were rows of shops and barbershops. We moved under the thick shade of the trees, to stay hidden from the eyes of passersby.

Thus, we reached the famous Khargush crossroads. We passed the crossroads and moved against the school walls. When we reached the school gate, the bell for the fourth lesson had already rung, and the streets were empty. Out of fear that someone might see us, we walked slowly along the walls behind the school building...

Behind the school was the sports field, where a schoolboys' soccer match was already in full swing. We moved along the walls with great caution and entered through small doors, reaching the building with the low-ceilinged classrooms.

To peek inside a classroom and watch a lesson, we had to climb onto the high foundation of the wall. We carefully placed our 'gifts' — those famous potties — on the ground. First, we peeked curiously into the first window, then the second. It was the turn for the third window. Already feeling free and confident, I held onto the window frame tightly and looked inside with great interest...

Unfortunately, this window happened to be right next to the teacher's desk. The sunlight coming from behind us cast the shadow of my head right on the window glass. The teacher immediately stopped speaking and directed his sharp gaze toward the window. At that second, my world seemed to stand still...

I was sure this was the window of some stranger teacher. But when my gaze fell to the other side of the glass, the world seemed to shake beneath my feet. The teacher sitting in the chair and now looking at me with amazement... was my own mother!

My mother, who was a teacher at that school, never expected to see her young son's face right outside the classroom window in the middle of a lesson. Salohiddin was still peeking into the second window with interest, unaware that our 'operation' had hit an invisible family wall...

At first, my mother's face turned white as a sheet from shock. When she regained her composure, she hurried out of the classroom and hugged me tightly.

Then the questioning began. It turned out that Salohiddin was also the son of teachers at the same school. In an instant, a 'parent-teacher meeting' gathered around us. At that very moment, the final bell rang, and the schoolyard filled with students...

One of the students pointed at the two potties sitting on the ground, drawing my mother's attention. My mother asked with amazement: 'What is this? Why did you bring these?'. She leaned over and lifted the lids of the potties. But what was inside shocked everyone — the potties were not empty! We had brought the 'result' of our last session in the kindergarten corridor to the school as a token of our freedom..."
